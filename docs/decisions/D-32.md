## D-32 — Warehouse residual root-caused: ground-floor Table 3.2.3.1 extent was misread (our defect, fixed)

End-use isolation on all four Warehouse variants showed the +2..+6% residual
was ENTIRELY heating (fans at parity, unlike the offices); the heat-balance
decomposition put 9.6 GJ/wk of it in opaque conduction; the object diff (its
envelope check only compared Outdoors surfaces — ground was a blind spot,
now understood) pinned it to the 4,598 m2 slab: legacy models it BARE
(U 7.39) in zones 4-7B while our reference insulated it FULL-AREA to the
Table 3.2.3.1 value (0.757 -> 0.863 construction-only).

The printed table [READ, MCP, 2020 and 2025 identical] is zone-conditional:
floors row reads "0.757 for 1.2 m" in zones 4-7B — a PERIMETER STRIP per
3.2.3.3.(3), the slab field carries NO prescriptive maximum — vs "0.379 for
full area" in zone 8. Confirmation from the fleet: at Yellowknife (zone 8)
legacy insulates full-area at U 0.403 = 0.379 with the interior film
stripped, agreeing with our 0.404 to three digits. Our full-area application
below zone 8 was the defect (8.4.4.1.(2): the reference must MEET 3.2, and
3.2 prescribes only the strip).

Decision: openstudio-envelope Prescriptive now implements the printed rule —
zone 8 keeps the full-area retarget; zones 4-7B keep the modeled slab field.
Kiva floors receive the strip through interior horizontal insulation (R sized
so the strip assembly meets the table U, width from the rules JSON). Simple
Ground floors receive the equivalent-UA representation recorded below. The
extent metadata
lives OUTSIDE u_values (which is structurally pinned to the legacy JSON by
test_data_integrity). Mechanism-parity test excludes ground floors (legacy
old-BTAP applies full-area — one of the two mutually inconsistent legacy
paths, see L-20).

Measured effect (January week, % of target): Toronto electric 106 -> 104
(heating delta +2.72 -> +1.28 GJ), Toronto gas 102 -> 106 (the reference's
gas-coil AFUE bin flipped across the 66 kW boundary as loads resized — the
new run is internally consistent at blended eff ~0.84), Edmonton 99 -> 101
(the load change legitimately tipped one ERV determination), Yellowknife
unchanged by construction (zone-8 path untouched). Remaining residual is
attributed to LEGACY-side divergences: the missing 1.2 m strip in the
archetypes (~6.7 GJ/wk of opaque loss on this slab, L-20 — Kiva 2D: the
strip recovers ~70% of full-slab insulation's benefit), ERV wheels running
in all climates from the wrong-vintage trigger (#2123 family, +0.19 GJ
parasitic), and CV fan character (+0.31 GJ, #2127 family). The fixed-point
premise again breaks from the legacy side, as with the D-28 offices.

### Applicability correction

The original correction conflated one physical realization with the Code
requirement. Sentence 3.2.3.3.(1) limits overall thermal transmittance.
Sentence (3) then requires insulation for **not less than** 1.2 m from the
perimeter **or over the full area**. It is a minimum placement extent, not a
requirement that only a two-dimensional solver can satisfy. Kiva remains the
higher-fidelity realization because it models the concentrated edge loss, but
simple `Ground` can establish a conforming reference target through a declared
equivalent-UA approximation.

The allocation comes directly from the Code. Article 3.4.1.2.(1), identical
in NECB 2020 and 2025, says that notwithstanding the performance path only
Article 3.2.1.1 and Subsection 3.2.4 continue to apply to the proposed
envelope. Article 3.2.3.3 is not in that list, so its perimeter strip is not a
requirement of the proposed building. Separately, Article 8.4.1.1.(1) requires
the building energy target to account for building components in accordance
with the Section 3.2 prescriptive requirements. The strip therefore belongs
in the reference target and not in the proposed. Article 8.4.1.2.(2) compares
the resulting annual energies.

Using one ground method on both sides is calculation-method normalization
under Articles 8.4.2.1, 8.4.2.2, 8.4.2.4 and 8.4.2.8, not correction of the
proposed building's performance. Where Kiva is selected and supported, the
performance path applies it to proposed and reference before proposed sizing.
The proposed keeps its submitted slab-field resistance and receives no
prescriptive strip; the reference clone receives the physical Kiva strip.
Where simple Ground is selected, or a valid simple-Ground construction cannot
be translated to Kiva without invented properties, both copies remain simple
and only the reference construction receives the equivalent-UA target.

For each simple-Ground floor the approximation uses one overall-U basis:

```text
Astrip = min(Afloor, exposed_perimeter * 1.2 m)
f = Astrip / Afloor
Ustrip = min(Ufield, Utable)
Ueq = f * Ustrip + (1 - f) * Ufield
```

`Astrip` is a declared area approximation, **not** an exact geometric band: it
can overcount overlapping corner regions and the model does not identify which
edge portions are exposed. `Ustrip = min(Ufield, Utable)` prevents a submitted
slab already better than the prescriptive value from being made worse. A
confirmed zero exposed edge gives `f = 0` and no reference conductance change.
Interior film resistance is handled consistently when the resulting overall U
is converted to construction conductance. The later D-35 lightweight rebuild
retains that conductance while supplying the reference mass and dynamic
response required by Articles 8.4.2.4 and 8.4.2.8; the approximation is not a
massless scalar replacement.

The scope remains narrower than "put Kiva on every ground floor." Zone 8 and
embedded floors take the full-area construction path. Unresolved
embedded-system applicability remains untouched rather than being guessed into
the ordinary-floor branch, and floors outside the reference envelope are
excluded by the same `inside_envelope` test as the reference transform.

The climate-dependent facts are unavailable to phase-1 validation, so phase 2b
performs a read-only inventory after HDD resolution and before changing any
floor. Preflight is limited to **method-neutral input defects** from which no
lawful reference representation can be derived: a missing parent space or
unusable/non-finite geometry, missing or non-thermal simple-Ground
construction, indeterminate area/perimeter, a `Foundation` boundary without
its foundation object, or an exact co-planar counterpart proving that a
`Ground` tag contradicts an interior adjacency. Absolute elevation alone is
not evidence: model coordinates may be shifted. Unknown no-mass materials and
air gaps are valid in layered simple-Ground constructions and are not input
defects merely because Kiva cannot accept them. A Kiva write or conversion
limitation likewise falls back to symmetric simple Ground rather than blaming
the submitted model. A genuine method-neutral defect raises
`compliance.PreflightError`; the CLI exits 3 before sizing or simulation and
names every offending floor. Selecting `simple` cannot bypass such a defect.

`GroundFCfactorMethod` needs a separate translation. Its F-factor represents
whole floor-plus-soil heat transfer per exposed perimeter, including effects
that are not the NECB assembly U; dimensional algebra such as
`F = Utable * area / perimeter` mixes unlike quantities. BTAP therefore
preserves a valid submitted F-factor construction and completes the requested
diagnostic lifecycle, but sets `compliant = null`,
`compliance_determination = "no_determination"` and CLI exit 6 until a
validated NECB F-factor applier exists. The same disposition applies to an
unsupported whole-foundation preprocessor method. This is a tool limitation,
not an input defect and not an instruction to replace a valid OSM method.

Simple Ground is no longer diagnostic-only: its equivalent-UA reference
normally establishes the target and can emit a determination after an annual
run. The acceptable-solution text decides the extent and neither the
approximation nor the interim factor-method tool gap is an AHJ referral or
alternative solution.

This preparation belongs only to the reference-building lifecycle. The NECB
2025 Article 8.4.4 archetype-EUI alternate path returns before
`prepare_model`, builds no reference building or 8.4.1.1 building energy
target, and therefore retains the proposed model's submitted ground method.
That exclusion is deliberate; it makes no ruling about how sensitive the
alternate path's absolute EUI metric may be to its ground model.
The reference-path `--eui-supplement` is different: it runs later in the same
reference lifecycle and receives the already Kiva-prepared proposed model, so
both its as-specified check and any normalized rerun start from that common
ground method.

For the known `CP02 CARPET PAD` no-mass representation, the compatibility
conversion uses the pinned oracle's thin-carpet properties: conductivity
0.06 W/(m.K), density 288 kg/m3 and specific heat 1380 J/(kg.K). It preserves
the layer's resistance and submitted absorptances while disclosing the added
heat capacity. Unknown no-mass materials and air gaps are not assigned
invented Kiva properties; they remain on simple Ground and use the
equivalent-UA reference path.

Setting monthly `Site:GroundTemperature:BuildingSurface` values is not a
substitute for Kiva's higher-fidelity soil domain: an EPW supplies weather, not
a uniquely prescribed building-surface ground-temperature series. It is also
unnecessary for the bounded simple-Ground approximation above.
