## D-107 — Authored sample fan coils carry the mode-specific sizing contract

**Decided:** every `ZoneHVAC:FourPipeFanCoil` in a SAMPLE MODEL this
repository authors is marked for D-106's mode-specific sizing contract before
that sample is saved, so each water coil rates at its own mode's design
airflow. The compliance pipeline does not infer or add the marker to fan coils
in an unmarked submitted model; a marker already authored into the input
remains effective.

Both narrowings matter. "A model this repository authors" would reach fixtures
and other artifacts, while this governs repository-authored SAMPLE models
only. And "a submitted model's fan coils are never marked" is literally false:
a generated BTAP sample can itself be submitted, and an input may already
carry the feature — translation honours an existing marker, and what the
pipeline does is simply not ADD one (Sol, `315`).

**Who/when:** Sol, 2026-10-24, on Claude's measurement of issue #68.

### The defect

EnergyPlus sizes a fan coil's maximum supply air on the LARGER of its heating
and cooling design flows, and sizes the cold water flow from the zone's
COOLING load. In a heating-dominated zone those disagree. Measured on sample
`04-fancoil-chiller`, Thermal Zone 3:

| | load | design airflow |
|---|---|---|
| heating | 11469 W | 0.59016 m³/s ← sets the unit maximum |
| cooling | 3388 W | 0.28137 m³/s ← the cold water flow follows this |

The coil is then asked to cool the heating-sized airflow at cooling-design
inlet conditions:

```
air-side load at 0.59016 m³/s    14506 W   (9301 sensible + 5205 latent)
water-side capacity at 6 K dT     6892 W
                                   2.10x   no UA satisfies it
at the zone's cooling airflow     6916 W   1.00x, feasible
```

EnergyPlus substitutes a UA and continues, so both affected samples wrote full
annual results with no fatal for three weeks.

Thermal Zones 1 and 3 are geometrically identical — 161.9 m², 152 m² exterior
wall, 40% WWR, same space type — and differ only in orientation. Zone 1's
cooling load is 7995 W against zone 3's 3388 W while their heating loads are
within 0.5%, so zone 1's cooling airflow happens to exceed its heating airflow
and stays feasible. The failure is an orientation lottery, not a system defect.

### Why this correction and not more chilled water

The larger airflow is the HEATING mode's requirement, not a cooling-load
signal. Raising the chilled water flow or the coil's capacity until the
inversion disappears would add cooling reserve, and 8.4.4.8.(2) caps the
reference building's cooling oversizing at the lesser of the proposed's
oversizing or 10% — so manufacturing reserve to clear a solver failure
corrupts the quantity that rule reads. Tables 8.4.4.7.-B and 8.4.5.7.-B
specify constant-volume fan control for Systems 2 and 5, and constant-volume
operation does not require a cooling coil to be rated at the heating mode's
design airflow.

D-106's correction leaves the design loads and water flows unchanged, keeps
the unit maximum at the larger mode, and rates each coil at its own mode. It
removes the UA failure WITHOUT adding reserve, which is why it survives the
oversizing cap.

The substituted UA does not make the proposed oversizing factor unreadable —
`_apply_oversizing_caps` takes it from `SizingParameters`, independently of
the coil inversion — so 8.4.4.8.(2) and 8.4.5.8.(2) remain applicable. What
the failed coil compromises is the capacity and part-load response that
8.4.2.10.(2) and (5) require the calculation to represent, and the proper
accounting of system capacities that 8.4.3.1.(2)(c) requires.

### Why `kind: process` and not `runtime`

A reader may expect `runtime`, because the marker changes shipped artifacts
and is read by product code at translation time. It is `process` because what
THIS decision governs is a repository authoring step: `generate_samples.py`
marking a coil before it writes an OSM. The product code that acts on the
marker — `btap.simulation.translation` — is D-106's, and D-106 carries the
`runtime` citation for it.

`test_every_runtime_entry_is_cited` made the distinction concrete: declared
`runtime`, D-107 failed with "no ruling literal cites them in python/btap",
because a script is not product source. The gate was right and the kind was
wrong.

### The boundary, which is the point of a separate decision

D-106 is deliberately REFERENCE-ONLY: it marks fan coils BTAP builds for the
reference building. This decision governs a different artifact — the sample
models this repository authors and ships — and shares only the translation
primitive.

Precisely: NEITHER D-106 NOR D-107 AUTHORIZES ADDING THE MARKER TO THE
SUBMITTED BUILDING AT PIPELINE ENTRY. D-106 marks only the new fan coils BTAP
builds FOR the reference; a D-107-authored sample already contains its own
marker before it is ever submitted. phylroy's ruling of 2026-10-21 is that we
cannot babysit people who have not done their due diligence on the proposed
building, and marking a submitter's own fan coil would re-size their
submission. Authoring a clean source artifact is not that. The pipeline's
policy is unchanged: an unmarked submitted fan coil still runs, and its Severe
is reported as `clean_run = false` rather than refused.

`5.2.1.1.(1)` requires HVAC sizing in accordance with good engineering
practice, and is the general obligation a solver fallback UA does not meet.

It is a STRUCTURED governing reference, which required refreshing the bounded
provisions index: the index had no entry for it in either edition, and
`generate_decisions.py` refused the citation with "has no committed evidence".
My first version carried it as prose instead. Sol ruled that insufficient
(`315`) on the precedent of his `286`: the bounded cache is generated FROM the
concrete governing references in the decision sources, so letting the current
cache boundary dictate a weaker authored record is backwards. A provenance gap
is to be closed, not worked around.

### Where a reader meets it

`python/scripts/generate_samples.py#_mark_fan_coils_for_mode_specific_sizing`
marks every fan coil in `save()`, and
`python/scripts/simulate_all_samples.py` refuses any authored sample that
sizes with a Severe or Fatal error. The catalogue sweep proves a SYSTEM on the
shared bare fixture and cannot prove the generated sample, which is the gap
issue #68 lived in for three weeks.
