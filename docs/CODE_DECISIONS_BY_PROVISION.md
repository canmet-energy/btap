interprets or implements it, what that decision decided, the counterpart in
the code's other editions, and the other codes and standards the requirement

[necb_decisions.md](necb_decisions.md).

Citations are identifiers and every decision is a paraphrase. This file
reproduces no Code text.

## Definitions

### Source

Every row is generated from the front matter of one canonical decision file,
`docs/decisions/D-NN.md`: its requirement keys and labels, its citations or
source records, and its decided text. Nothing is authored in this file. To
change a row, edit that decision file and regenerate.

### Provenance

How a citation reached this file, and what was checked:

- **Structured source records.** Each NECB reference is validated offline
  against its edition's provision index,
  `python/btap/codes/necb/data/<code id>/coverage/provisions.json`, which is
  built from authorized Codes-service payloads retained under the same
  edition's `provenance/` directory. A source that no registered index covers
  is carried as authored.
- **Established legacy mappings.** Each citation must match the citation
  grammar, and a Section 8.4 citation must also exist in its edition's
  `coverage/articles_8_4.json`. A citation outside Section 8.4 is carried as
  authored.
- **Not yet attributed.** Citations are recorded as authored and checked
  against nothing.

### Status

| Status | Where it appears | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Governing | A primary row under its code and edition | The source record that establishes the provision for that edition. Only this role creates a primary row. |
| Incorporated reference, related | Other sources | A cross-reference to a code or standard. It never establishes where a decision applies and never creates a primary row. |
| Transitional | A primary row whose decided text is marked Transitional | The edition and the exact citations are an established mapping, but the requirement has no decided text of its own yet. The decision-wide summary stands in for it, printed once under Transitional decision summaries, until the decision is converted to the structured form. |
| Not yet attributed | Edition not yet attributed, the last section | The decision records citations but no edition has been established for them. It is never placed under an edition. |

### Reading a row

A row is one requirement in one edition. **Provision** lists that
requirement's citations in the spelling the decision records. **Other
editions** lists the governing citations of the same requirement in the
code's other editions. **Other sources** lists incorporated and related
references.

Rows are ordered by code, then edition, then the row's first provision, then
decision number, then requirement key. A provision orders by division, then its
number read numerically (8.4.4.9 before 8.4.4.10), then its kind: an Article
with its Sentences, Clauses and Subclauses, then a Table with its notes, then
an Appendix note. An Article and a Table that share a number are different
provisions and stay adjacent.

## Transitional decision summaries

A row marked **Transitional** has no decided text of its own yet, so it points here.
Each summary below is the decision-wide paraphrase from the registry, printed once because it covers the whole decision and would otherwise repeat on every row. It is not a per-requirement decision.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-27"></a>

**[D-27](necb_decisions.md#d-27)** — Service-water circulators are outside the hydronic pump article; transfer head reconciled

The pump-power transfer had been applying a space-heating power intensity to service-water circulators, which combined with an inherited high head implies an impossible pump efficiency and is fatal to the simulation engine. Loops carrying a water heater or water-use connections are now outside the hydronic pump scope entirely, and are also excluded from the proposed-side intensity statistics. Every remaining transfer carries a physicality guard that reconciles head, with a loud warning, when the implied efficiency is impossible. SUPERSEDED IN PART BY D-92, 2026-09-16: that head-reconciliation mechanism is gone, not suppressed — the reference pump is now STATED as rated head, design shaft power per unit flow per unit head and motor efficiency, with rated power left autosized, so there is no computed power to repair and \`\_reconcile\_pump\_head\` and \`DESIGN\_PUMP\_EFFICIENCY\` no longer exist. The preceding sentence is the 2026-07-25/29 account and is retained as period evidence. What SURVIVES of D-27, and what D-92 and the resizing boundary still rest on, is the scope ruling: a loop carrying a water heater or water-use connections is outside Article 8.4.x.14 entirely and is excluded from the proposed-side intensity statistics.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-38"></a>

**[D-38](necb_decisions.md#d-38)** — Prescriptive pump-power caps applied min-wins over the intensity transfer

Reference hydronic pump power is set by the proposed-intensity transfer and then clamped at the prescriptive maximum watts per kilowatt of the loop's peak thermal demand, because the prescriptive requirement is a ceiling the reference building may never pierce. Taking the smaller of the two is the only reading that satisfies both articles at once, and it removes the perverse incentive whereby wasteful proposed pumping inflates its own target. Unsized inputs produce an audited not-evaluable note rather than a silent skip.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-39"></a>

**[D-39](necb_decisions.md#d-39)** — Two-pipe fan-coil reference heating honoured conditionally

The selection table lists this reference system with no heating, while a separate sentence requires heating presence per thermal block to match the proposed. The reconciliation: a cooled-but-unheated proposed block gets the table-literal cooling-only reference, and a heated block makes the reference MODEL heating under the presence override — a hot-water loop, boiler and coils are BUILT inside a four-pipe fan-coil surrogate, which the Code does not select, so AHJ-11 refers the precedence and AHJ-18 records the surrogate's missing plant-side changeover (Sol, 129). Edition-verified against both texts by Sol's 126. A block with no conditioning at all still gets no reference system, since that is absence rather than a stated none.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-46"></a>

**[D-46](necb_decisions.md#d-46)** — Staged heating and cooling as unitary multispeed coils, stage count set after sizing

The code requires the reference building's furnaces and direct-expansion systems to be modelled as equal capacity stages, two of them up to a threshold capacity and one per threshold increment above it. Reference packaged single-zone systems now build their fan and coils inside a unitary container holding multi-stage coils, because the simulation engine cannot place a multi-stage coil directly on an air loop; a pass that runs after sizing sets only the NUMBER of stages from the sized total, leaving every stage capacity automatically sized so that capacity auto-iteration keeps working. Equal increments are realized through the container's per-stage airflow ratios, and minimum-efficiency values are looked up once against the unit's total capacity and written to every stage.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-47"></a>

**[D-47](necb_decisions.md#d-47)** — Stage count clamped at the simulation engine's four-stage ceiling, with a shouted warning

The multi-stage coil objects in the simulation engine cannot hold more than four stages, so very large equipment cannot be given the number of equal stages the code asks for. The stage count is clamped at four and every clamp raises a loud audit warning naming the required count, the sized capacity and the ceiling, so that oversized cases are enumerated rather than hidden. Splitting the load across extra parallel units was rejected because it would change the reference system topology to work around a tool limit.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-48"></a>

**[D-48](necb_decisions.md#d-48)** — Staging applies to air-loop unitary equipment only; zone terminals and tempering coils stay single-speed

Equipment staging is applied to air-loop unitary equipment only. Packaged terminal units keep single-speed coils because the simulation engine's input schema forbids a multi-stage coil there even though the modelling toolkit accepts the assignment, and make-up-air tempering coils temper a ventilation stream rather than being the staged unitary equipment the sentences describe. Every skip is reported in the audit grouped by reason with a count, rather than one entry per coil, so that a building with hundreds of identical terminals does not swamp the report.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-49"></a>

**[D-49](necb_decisions.md#d-49)** — Electric resistance heating is not a furnace, so the furnace staging sentence does not reach it

The staging sentence governs furnaces, and an electric resistance heating coil is not one: it has no burner, no combustion staging, and linear part-load behaviour, so dividing it into equal stages would add objects without changing anything physical. Electric-heated reference packaged systems therefore pair the staged multi-speed cooling coil, which the cooling article does require, with a plain single-stage electric heating coil in the same unitary container.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-50"></a>

**[D-50](necb_decisions.md#d-50)** — Terminal and secondary capacity split accounted through dedicated-outdoor-air zone sizing

Where the selection table places heating or cooling in both a zone terminal and a secondary system, the terminal is to be sized for the space loads while the pair together covers the peak. For the reference systems that pair a zone terminal with a make-up-air unit this is expressed directly as dedicated-outdoor-air zone-sizing accounting with a neutral supply-air strategy, so the ventilation stream neither adds nor removes zone load and the terminal is sized on the space load alone. Reference systems that mix outdoor air into the supply stream have no equivalent accounting, so their split is only approximated by ordinary mixed-air zone sizing and each such group is audited as an approximation.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-51"></a>

**[D-51](necb_decisions.md#d-51)** — Reference daylighting photocontrols are built by default

The reference building is required to carry the daylighting photocontrols that the prescriptive lighting-control requirements mandate, and the effect of those controls must be evaluated in the reference. The pipeline therefore builds them on every reference-path run by default and evaluates them with detailed daylighting calculations, because a reference generated without them over-states reference lighting energy and hands the proposed building an energy target more lenient than the code sets. A caller may still switch the photocontrols off, and a run that does so is warned in the audit that the requirement went unevaluated rather than being left silent.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-52"></a>

**[D-52](necb_decisions.md#d-52)** — The reference heat pump's auxiliary-heating energy type is elected from the proposed building's annual run

The heat-pump reference system's terminal or auxiliary heating must take the energy type used for terminal or auxiliary heating of the thermal blocks the heat pump serves that has the largest annual energy use, provided the heat pump itself carries more than a third of those blocks' total annual space-heating energy. That is an annual-energy question, so the proposed building's annual simulation is now run BEFORE the reference is built - it depends on nothing downstream, so the reordering costs no additional simulation - and its per-equipment delivered heating energy is aggregated by energy type over the blocks each heat pump serves: the pump's own blocks for an air-source machine, the blocks of every pump sharing the same source water loop for a water- or ground-source machine. All quantities compared are delivered heat, one consistent basis across energy types. Where the election cannot run - no annual data because only sizing was requested, blocks with no terminal or auxiliary heating at all, or a heat-pump share at or below the threshold so the sentence's proviso is unmet - the structural energy-type proxy that follows the proposed system's fuels elects instead, and the audit states plainly which path elected the fuel. The election is made once, from the first annual run, and is not re-litigated by later capacity iterations, which adjust sizes but never rebuild the reference topology. The clause forcing electricity for a pump that is neither air-, water- nor ground-source is only applied when such a source is affirmatively established, which the current source taxonomy never produces, so an evidence-less detection keeps the proxy rather than guessing. The cooling-capacity clause - peak cooling load without oversizing - was verified by measurement rather than assumption: the per-zone unit cooling sizing factor the heat-pump builders stamp overrides the model-wide capped factor, giving identical sized capacity with the global factor at its cap versus at unity.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-54"></a>

**[D-54](necb_decisions.md#d-54)** — The proposed's demand-control ventilation strategy is carried across the reference teardown, but its peak-rate outdoor-air method is not

The reference building is required to be modelled with the same demand-control ventilation strategies the proposed building implements, but the reference air systems are rebuilt from scratch and their fresh outdoor-air controllers start with demand control switched off, so a proposed building with demand control was being compared against a reference that ventilated at full design rate whenever its fans ran. The characterization step now records each proposed air loop's demand-control setting before the old systems are torn out, and the rebuilt reference controllers receive the same setting. The simulation engine states demand control as a flag plus an outdoor-air method, and only the methods that are themselves demand-control schemes — carbon-dioxide sensing and occupancy-proportional control — are copied, because the remaining methods determine the PEAK ventilation rate, which a different sentence governs and which the reference already inherits from the cloned space ventilation specifications.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-56"></a>

**[D-56](necb_decisions.md#d-56)** — Reference fan-coil systems get a real water-side economizer, and the cooling-tower setpoint reset is what makes it operate

The reference selection table sends the fan-coil systems to the indirect, water-side form of cooling with outdoor air rather than to the air-side economizer, and that requirement was previously only warned about, leaving the reference with no economizer at all while the quality-assurance checker flagged the same system for lacking one. A plate heat exchanger is now built between the heat-rejection loop and the chilled-water loop, sized from the loop's full design cooling load and modulating onto the chilled-water loop's own design supply temperature. Because the reference rejects heat through an evaporative cooling tower, the wet-bulb criterion is the sentence that governs and the sensible-transfer criterion is recorded as inapplicable; the tower setpoint must also be reset to track the outdoor wet-bulb plus the tower's design approach, since the builder otherwise pins the loop at its design heat-rejection temperature and no exchanger placed there could ever operate.

<a id="transitional-summary-d-57"></a>

**[D-57](necb_decisions.md#d-57)** — Reference daylighting photocontrols follow the current daylight-responsive control requirement, not the superseded one

Where the reference building must carry daylight-responsive photocontrols is now decided by the current requirement rather than by criteria carried over from a superseded edition of the code. The current requirement gates each space type through the prescriptive lighting-control table, then tests the input power of the general lighting inside the daylighted areas against fixed wattage thresholds - one for the primary sidelighted areas, a higher one for primary plus secondary, and a separate one for the areas under skylights - and treats the sidelighting and toplighting requirements as INDEPENDENT of one another. The superseded criteria were area and effective-aperture tests applied conjunctively, so a space lit only by windows failed on its zero skylight area and never received photocontrols at all, which left the reference building carrying fewer controls than the code requires and therefore set an energy target more lenient than the code writes. Because the lighting power density is uniform across each space, the input power inside a daylighted area is exactly the density times that area, so no luminaire layout is needed. The daylighted areas themselves are now built as unioned polygons so overlapping areas are not double-counted, a secondary sidelighted area is computed at all, and the code's own precedence - a primary sidelighted area is never reduced by a skylight, while a secondary area yields to both - is applied. The published exceptions are honoured, including the exemption of retail spaces from the sidelighting requirement and of low-transmittance skylights and high-latitude buildings with little lighting power from the toplighting requirement. Two independent extractions of the prescriptive control table are each partly corrupted and disagree with one another, so the vendored control matrix records a state per space type rather than a bare flag: columns that cannot be read are warned about and take a documented conservative default that tightens rather than loosens the target, and space types the table does not list at all are determined from the code text to fall outside the requirement instead of being guessed at. The superseded rule remains selectable so that the parity gate against the legacy implementation stays callable, and selecting it warns that the resulting target is looser than the code requires.

**[D-92](necb_decisions.md#d-92)** — The reference pump is stated as head, efficiency and motor efficiency; EnergyPlus derives its power

Decided: 2026-09-16 under D-10 delegation, after Sol's D-58 scope ruling and two Fable reviews (DF-11 increment A). The reference pump is STATED, not computed-then-repaired: the efficiency pass writes its rated head, its design shaft power per unit flow per unit head and its motor efficiency, and leaves rated power AUTOSIZED so EnergyPlus derives the power. D-11's value source is unchanged here (the proposed loop-type's combined W/(L/s)); what is replaced is the mechanism. The requirement. NECB 2020 8.4.4.14 (2025: 8.4.5.14, identical text): (1) each reference hydronic pump has a total static head and efficiency identical to that of the CORRESPONDING proposed pump; (2) where the proposed uses more than one pump in a given hydronic system, the reference pump's peak SHAFT power demand is identical to the combined peak shaft power demand of the proposed's pumps; (3) where the proposed pump's head or efficiency is NOT KNOWN, the reference pump is based on the proposed's peak power demand in W/(L/s). Note A-8.4.4.14.(2) (explanatory) gives the method for (2): preserve the peak power by ADJUSTING THE PUMP HEAD, at a flow-weighted average pump efficiency. 8.4.4.9.(6)(f) separately fixes the reference plant's peak pumping flow (installed capacity, pure water, 16 C drop), so under (2) both flow and shaft power are article-owned and head is the only free variable. Verified against the codes MCP; the note's own example computes its 179.4 L/min from (6)(f) at 200 kW. What was wrong. The pass hard-set rated power to intensity x reference flow and then bent the inherited head whenever the resulting triple implied a pump efficiency above the motor efficiency (the D-27 reconciliation), because EnergyPlus FATALS on "Calculated Pump Efficiency \> 100%".
